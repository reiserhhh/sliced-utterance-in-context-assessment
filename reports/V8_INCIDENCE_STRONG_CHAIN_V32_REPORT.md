## Question

Can one aggregate author-pair recurrence matrix distinguish a true
simultaneous six-author response core from ten independently persistent,
overlapping triplets whose aggregate pair recurrence is identical?

## Construction

The strong chain uses a \(2-(6,3,2)\) balanced block design. Each of six
authors occurs in five triplets and each author pair occurs in exactly two
triplets. Repeating every triplet over five conditions gives every author 25
active opportunities and every pair 10 close co-occurrences.

The matched core world gives every author the same 25 opportunities, but all
six authors enter one common ball together over ten conditions. The designed
pair recurrence matrices are exactly equal:

\[
W^{\mathrm{core}}_{ij}=W^{\mathrm{chain}}_{ij}.
\]

The Gram reservoir and paired Procrustes noise also make integrated whole-map
distances algebraically equal.

Every strong-chain triplet independently exceeds the frozen persistence
threshold:

\[
P_3=\frac5{65}\frac{21}{22}=0.0734>0.04,
\]

while the positive six-author core has

\[
P_6=\frac{10}{65}\frac{18}{22}=0.1259.
\]

## Correct interpretation

The graph outputs in the strong-chain world are not wrong for their actual
estimand. Six authors really do form a stable aggregate pair-recurrence
cluster. They are wrong only if that cluster is reinterpreted as a statement
that all six authors converged simultaneously.

V3.2 therefore establishes an information-loss result:

> Compressing condition-indexed pair recurrence into one aggregate
> \(W_{ij}\) makes simultaneous six-author convergence indistinguishable from
> a matched collection of overlapping persistent triplets. Retaining
> condition identity permits the frozen no-chaining estimator to refuse the
> simultaneous-core interpretation.

It does not establish that hypergraphs are superior to every graph method.
A condition-aware graph receives the missing concurrency information and can
distinguish the current worlds.

## What this says about intersections

An informative “intersection” is not a high-degree node in an aggregate
network. Its possible higher-order content is the simultaneous feasibility
of one common response neighborhood for a particular author set under the
same condition. Pairwise proximity aggregated over time cannot determine
whether all relations co-occurred.

That is still a mathematical response-structure statement, not a personality
statement. The same geometry can be generated by prompts, opportunities, or
experimental assignment.
