## Mathematical interpretation

For author set \(H\), condition \(z\), and view \(v\), V3.5 estimates the
common-ball birth scale

\[
b_{H,z,v}=\rho_{\mathrm{MEB}}(X_{H,z,v})/r_{z,v},
\qquad
b^\cap_{H,z}=\max_v b_{H,z,v}.
\]

This turns finite radius-hit counting into an exact scale margin. The
restricted maximum-statistic permutation test then asks whether recurrent
membership exceeds groups matched on condition and opportunity structure.
Local first- and second-order geometry separates the registered ordinary
transverse, tangent-contact, coincident, near-miss, and boundary templates.

## What passed, and what did not

The PASS means that the registered estimators recover deliberately separated
geometric mechanisms under the registered low-noise simulator. The near
perfect results indicate a clean identifiability battery, not realistic
difficulty. They do **not** establish that:

- embedding trajectories in human text are smooth response manifolds;
- a common ball is a thought, personality, affinity, or intelligence node;
- the thresholds transfer outside the planted worlds;
- this method adds information beyond a complete continuous distance matrix;
- any result is clinically interpretable.

The nonlinear AUC uses the same local rank score that defines transversality;
it does not reconstruct an unknown nonlinear manifold. Likewise, the reported
intersection dimension is assigned from the classified local template rather
than independently estimated from a general Jacobian. These are construct
scope limitations, not label leakage. The accepted result is exact birth
scale, the registered identity-permutation max-T procedure, and low-noise
local jet-template discrimination.

geometric junction, does the event-level transition kernel distinguish
